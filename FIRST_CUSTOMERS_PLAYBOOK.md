# DealPilot — First Customers Playbook

Built with the `founderos:find-first-customers` method (its reference file `early-sales.md` was read for the templates). Goal: the first ~10 paying customers, won by hand. Reddit is out of scope (the owner handles it separately).

## Inputs used (change any that are wrong)

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Product | DealPilot: paste a client brief, get an editable proposal draft, send a branded link, collect an e-signature and a Stripe deposit. Free plan 5 proposals a month; Pro $12 a month ($9 billed yearly). |
| Who it's for (ICP) | Freelance designers and web developers who send proposals regularly to direct clients |
| The problem | Writing each proposal costs an evening, and clients go quiet after it is sent |
| Constraint | Solo founder, about 8 hours a week, no automation at this stage |
| Conversion metric | Contacted → replied → call → trial → paid |

**Who is NOT the ICP (disqualify quickly):** people who only bid on platforms where the platform sets the proposal format, people who send fewer than one proposal a month, and agencies with an established sales stack. Their pain is lower or different.

## 1. The target list: 30 to 50 named, reachable people

**I did not create this list.** Real names must come from real sources you check yourself. Build it in the table in section 6. Where to look (from the method, adapted):

1. **Your network's network.** Ask people you know for warm intros to freelance designers or developers, by name. Warm intros convert better than cold messages. {unverified}
2. **Places you already belong** (professional groups, Slack or Discord communities you're in, local meetups). Join by contributing; don't drop links.
3. **Search by job title.** "Freelance brand designer", "freelance web developer", "independent UX designer" on LinkedIn, plus portfolio sites you can browse publicly.
4. **People who talk about the problem publicly.** Someone who posted about proposals, pricing or ghosting clients. Reference that post in your message.
5. **Public reviews of competing tools.** People who publicly say what they dislike about Bonsai, HoneyBook, Proposify or PandaDoc are already looking. Read them to understand the pain; contact only if they are also a clear fit and use public contact routes.

For each person write: name, where you found them, and one line on **why this person**. If you can't write the line, skip them. Aim for 30 to 50, then message 10 a week.

## 2. One channel: LinkedIn direct messages (plus warm intros)

Reason: it is where freelancers' professional profiles are, you can personalise from their real work, and you can do it by hand in the time budget. This is a judgment call, not measured. {unverified} Switch to email only if people prefer it in the first conversations. Don't run two channels until one has produced replies.

Rules: no links in the first message (reads as spam, per the method). One message per person, sent by hand. Follow each platform's messaging limits and terms.

## 3. The message (5 sentences or fewer)

**Good (pick one, personalise the first line):**

> Hi [Name], I saw [specific true detail: their project, post, or how they describe their work]. I'm talking to freelance [designers/developers] about how they write proposals, and what happens after they send them. I'm not selling anything, just learning. Would you be open to a 15-minute chat this week?

> Hi [Name], your [project/post] caught my eye. I'm building a tool that drafts proposals from a client brief, and I'd like to learn how you handle that today. Could I ask you three questions on a 15-minute call?

**Bad (avoid):**

> Hi there! I hope this finds you well! We're an innovative AI-powered platform that revolutionises proposals with 10x speed. Our features include X, Y, Z. Can I book 30 minutes to walk you through a full demo? [link]

Why it fails: not personal, about us, a feature dump, big ask, hype words, a link.

Write three versions, send the tightest. Test one change per batch of ten, never after a single reply.

## 4. The conversation (15 minutes)

**Listen first.** Ask, then confirm they have the problem:

1. "Walk me through how you handle proposals today. What tools or templates do you use?"
2. "How long does the last one take you, start to send?"
3. "What happens after you send it? How often do clients go quiet?"
4. "How much does that cost you in time, or in lost work?"
5. "Is this a now problem or a someday problem?"

If they don't have the problem or won't prioritise it, thank them and move on.

**The ask (make exactly one):** if the problem is real and current:

> "Would you like me to build a proposal for your next real lead? Paste the brief into DealPilot, you edit it, and tell me what's wrong. It's on the free plan, so it costs nothing. If it's useful, Pro is $12 a month."

**Name the next step and a date:** "I'll set you up today. Can we talk Thursday about what you changed?" A vague "let's stay in touch" is a polite no.

**End every call with:** "Who else do you know who has this problem?"

Do things that don't scale on purpose: set up their account for them, run their first brief with them, answer in minutes. Treat their feedback as the product roadmap.

## 5. Objections (be honest, log the reason)

| They say | What it usually means | Honest response |
|---|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | Value not clear yet, or wrong person | Remind them the free plan costs nothing; ask what an hour of their time is worth; if still no, wrong ICP |
| "No time right now" | Low priority | "When does this become urgent for you?" If never, disqualify |
| "Send me info" | Soft no | Offer the 15-minute call; send a one-pager only if they name a next step |
| "I already use [tool]" | Has an alternative | Ask what it doesn't do well; if nothing, not your customer yet |
| "Let me think about it" | Unspoken doubt | "What would you need to see to feel confident?" |
| "Does it use my data to train AI?" | Trust | Answer only from facts you have verified about your product; don't guess |

Three "too expensive" in a row is a pricing or positioning signal, not three coincidences.

Stages: Contacted → Replied → Call → Trial → Paid. Review weekly:
- Few replies → the list or the message needs work.
- Replies but no calls → the ask is too big or the message too vague.
- Calls but no trials → the problem isn't real, or the ICP is wrong.
- Trials but no paid → onboarding or pricing.

No numeric targets until you have 30 messages of baseline.

## 7. Do not

- Blast a templated sequence to strangers before closing one customer by hand.
- Pitch before listening, or only collect "feedback chats" and never make the ask.
- Argue with a "no". Log the reason and move on.
- Chase polite replies from people who don't have the problem.
- Keep this in your head. Use the table, so you can see the real conversion.
- Message people through channels they didn't make public, or add them to mailing lists without consent.
